Dear Editors-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, "The Bottleneck Effect: When Small-Model Scaling Fails for Code Generation," for publication as an original research article in Innovations in Systems and Software Engineering: A NASA Journal.

The manuscript studies a software-engineering reliability problem in neural code generation: why similarly sized small language models can differ sharply in executable-code behavior. Rather than treating benchmark scores as a single leaderboard number, the paper traces failures across benchmark families, prompt formats, decoding choices, layer interventions, and artifact provenance.

The paper fits the journal's emphasis on systems and software engineering because it analyzes code-generation systems as engineered pipelines whose reliability depends on model family, pretraining distribution, generation protocol, evaluation strictness, and failure-mode structure. The central claim is deliberately conservative: in the small-model regime studied here, parameter count alone is a weak predictor of executable-code reliability, while code-specific pretraining and distributed execution-relevant representations provide stronger explanatory evidence.

The submission includes a reviewer-facing artifact package with processed tables, benchmark summaries, result artifacts, scripts, configuration files, figures, and a claim-to-artifact audit. These materials are intended to make the empirical claims traceable and reproducible.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
