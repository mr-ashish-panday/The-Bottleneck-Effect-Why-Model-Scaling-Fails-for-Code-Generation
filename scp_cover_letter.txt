Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "The Bottleneck Effect: When Small-Model Scaling Fails for Code Generation," for publication as an original research article in Science of Computer Programming.

The manuscript studies a software-development reliability problem in neural code generation: why similarly sized small language models can differ sharply in executable-code behavior. Rather than treating benchmark scores as a single leaderboard number, the paper traces failures across benchmark families, prompt formats, decoding choices, layer interventions, and artifact provenance.

The paper fits Science of Computer Programming because it connects program generation, executable correctness, validation, testing, and programming-language evaluation. Its central claim is deliberately conservative: in the small-model regime studied here, parameter count alone is a weak predictor of executable-code reliability, while code-specific pretraining, generation protocol, and the distribution of execution-relevant computation across layers provide stronger explanatory evidence.

The submission includes a reviewer-facing artifact package with processed tables, benchmark summaries, result artifacts, scripts, configuration files, figures, and a claim-to-artifact audit. These materials are intended to make the empirical claims traceable and reproducible.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
