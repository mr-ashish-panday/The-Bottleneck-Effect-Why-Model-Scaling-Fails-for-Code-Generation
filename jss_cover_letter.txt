Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, "The Bottleneck Effect: When Small-Model Scaling Fails
for Code Generation," for publication as a regular research article in the
Journal of Systems and Software.

This paper studies a practical software-engineering problem: why small language
models that appear similar in size can differ sharply in code-generation
reliability. Instead of treating benchmark accuracy as a single leaderboard
number, the manuscript traces failures across benchmarks, prompt formats,
decoding choices, layer interventions, and artifact provenance.

The manuscript should fit JSS because it contributes empirical evidence about
AI-based software generation systems, their evaluation reliability, and the
engineering choices that shape whether generated code executes correctly. The
main result is not a generic "bigger is better" scaling claim. The evidence
shows that code-generation reliability depends on model family, code-specific
pretraining, benchmark distribution, prompt interface, decoding policy, and the
metric used to summarize generated programs.

The submission includes a reproducible artifact trail: processed result tables,
run logs, plotting scripts, benchmark summaries, and figure-generation scripts.
These artifacts are intended to make the reported claims auditable rather than
dependent on manually transcribed results.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
